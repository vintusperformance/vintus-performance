## 1. Alex Hormozi

**Access level: high.** By far the most publicly documented person on this
list — he has written entire books (`$100M Offers`, `$100M Leads`) that
explicitly teach his own hook/CTA/sales methodology. Treat this section as the
most reliable in the file.

- **Hook style:** Talking-head, direct-to-camera, high eye contact, minimal
  set dressing. His own stated framework is **Hook → Retain → Reward**: grab
  attention with a bold claim tailored to the platform, retain interest with
  an unresolved question or story loop, reward attention by actually
  delivering on the hook. Short videos (~30s), high cut/caption density
  (flashing text, color changes, B-roll inserts) to sustain watch-through.
- **Psychology tactics:** Heavy, explicit use of scarcity and loss aversion
  ("scarcity stacks," "everyday urgency blueprint"), social proof (testimonials,
  user counts), reciprocity (giving away real frameworks for free to earn
  attention), and specificity as a credibility signal — his content is built
  around numbered, structured frameworks (the Value Equation, the
  problem-solution matrix) rather than vague claims.
- **CTA pattern:** Value delivered first and completely (his stated
  philosophy is that free content should be genuinely complete, not a teaser)
  — the CTA is typically a low-friction next step (follow, link in bio, "get
  the book"), not a hard pitch inside every single video. The hard sell lives
  downstream, in the funnel his content feeds, not in the hook video itself.
- **Speech/delivery:** Blunt, plain-language, declarative. Deliberately
  strips jargon out and replaces it with numbered frameworks and analogies.
  High energy but not manic — controlled intensity, not hype-stacking.
- **Sales mechanics:** The clearest documented "value stack" mechanic in this
  set — bundle a core offer with complements that solve the *next* problem
  the buyer will hit, add a bonus that removes a specific objection, and make
  the bonuses alone feel worth the price so the core offer feels free.
  Objection handling is systematized: agree with the objection, explain why
  the premium option exists, restate value, ask if they want the real
  solution or the cheap one. This is a documented, aggressive close pattern —
  not soft.

## 3. Alex Eubank (@alexeubank / natural bodybuilder)

**Access level: moderate-high.** Well covered by fitness press given his
scale (~2.8M IG, ~1.2M YouTube).

- **Hook style:** Physique-first — the aesthetic body itself is the visual
  hook before he speaks. Content spans vlogs, workout footage, and

- **Psychology tactics:** Authority through visible physical proof (the
  physique as evidence the method works) plus authenticity/identity framing

  both function as trust differentiators against a market full of
  enhanced-but-undisclosed physiques. Audience is explicitly young men 18-30.

  codes and sponsor products (RawGear, Alpha Lion, Bucked Up, Celsius) rather

  (Elysium Athletic) and, more recently, ownership of a supplement brand

  Monetization is diversified across sponsorships, owned brand, and
  programs rather than one funnel. This is a fundamentally different model
  than a service business: his "product" and his "content" are the same
  thing (his physique), which doesn't map cleanly onto a coaching service.

both likely creator-economy/personal-brand comps, not fitness comps), and

**itsjoao** — handle confirmed but content niche unresolved, and direct

### Cross-cutting patterns (the actual creator-economy playbook, structurally)

These hold across every well-documented account here, and they're
mechanical, not just "they're all confident on camera":

1. **The hook is engineered, not organic.** Every documented creator treats
   the first 1-3 seconds as a distinct, deliberately authored unit — a claim,
   a question, or a contrarian statement, built before the rest of the
   script. Georgas literally sells a compiled hook library; Hormozi names it
   as step one of a three-part framework. This isn't a personality trait,
   it's a production step.
2. **Free content is engineered to feel complete, not like a teaser.** The
   paid conversion happens *downstream* of the free content (an app, a
   course, a DM funnel), not inside the free content itself via constant
   pitching. The free layer's job is trust-building; the funnel's job is
   converting.
3. **Identity/in-group framing does real work.** "Hot Girl Sales Club,"
   natural-bodybuilding-plus-faith identity, "become the man your future
   needs" — these aren't taglines, they're audience self-selection
   mechanisms. The follower isn't just consuming content, they're joining a
   labeled group.
4. **Specificity substitutes for vagueness as the primary trust lever.**
   Numbered frameworks, named steps, exact dollar or follower figures,
   named programs. Vague inspiration is the amateur version of this content
   category; specificity is what separates the professional operators from
   noise.
5. **High posting cadence is universal among the documented accounts.**
   Daily or near-daily. None of this works as a monthly or even weekly
   cadence — the format depends on volume.
6. **CTA is present but disciplined, not constant.** Even Hormozi — the most
   sales-literate person in this set — doesn't hard-pitch every video. The
   ask is usually a low-friction next step (follow, link in bio) with the
   harder sale reserved for a smaller number of dedicated moments or an
   entirely separate funnel layer.

### Flatly incompatible with Vintus brand voice — do not adopt

Cross-referencing against CLAUDE.md's Brand Voice and Standing Rules
sections, several tactics that clearly work for these creators should be
named and explicitly rejected, not quietly avoided:

- **Manufactured urgency/scarcity stacking** (Hormozi's documented
  "scarcity stacks," "everyday urgency blueprint"). CLAUDE.md is explicit:
  no manufactured urgency that isn't true, and no fabricated scarcity
  ("3 spots left") unless the number is real. This is a hard no regardless
  of how well it performs for Hormozi's audience.
- **Dollar-figure and lifestyle-flex proof** (Sapp's reported revenue
  figures, the car, visible income signaling as implicit social proof).
  Vintus has no verified client results yet and CLAUDE.md bans fabricating
  any — but even setting fabrication aside, lifestyle-flex is a status
  appeal fundamentally at odds with a premium-not-volume, restraint-coded
  brand. It's the wrong psychological lever even where it would be honest.
- **Physique-as-product** (Eubank's model, where the aesthetic body is the
  hook and the proof simultaneously). CLAUDE.md states this directly:
  "Executives don't want abs." Adopting a physique-first hook style would
  directly contradict the positioning Anthony has already settled on.
- **High-emotional-intensity aspirational identity language** (Pruett's
  "become the man your future needs," "1% better every day," faith-legacy
  framing). Not wrong on its own terms, but it's a warmer, more
  motivational-poster register than "a seasoned D1 strength coach speaking
  in private conversation." CLAUDE.md's sentence-discipline rule — "if a
  line could appear on any fitness account, it's wrong" — rules this out.
- **Aggressive, scripted objection-handling closes** (Hormozi's
  agree-explain-restate-ask sequence). This is a legitimate sales
  discipline for `sales-agent` to study for actual sales calls, but it does
  not belong in public comment threads or captions — it reads as pressure,
  not precision, for this audience. The ICP is explicitly described as
  "turned off by hype" and having "low tolerance for being talked down to."
- **Ecommerce-style value-stack bonus-stuffing** ("bonuses that alone
  justify the price so the core feels free"). Vintus sells one clear
  service tier at a defined price with defined inclusions (CLAUDE.md's
  pricing table). Stacking artificial bonuses to obscure the real value math
  is an info-product mechanic, not a fit for a premium single-tier service.

### Structural/psychological tactics that ARE adoptable — because they're mechanics, not tone

The distinction that matters: reject the *emotional register* these
creators use, keep the *underlying structural mechanism* where it's
tone-neutral.

- **Engineered hooks as a production discipline, not an accident.**
  `day-01-scripts.md` already produced six distinct hook types (blunt
  statement, direct question, scenario, number-framed, contrarian claim,
  analogy) without anyone naming them as a deliberate taxonomy. Formalizing
  this as a checklist `script-writer` works from — one script per hook type,
  every day, not just Day 1 — is a direct, brand-safe adoption of what every
  documented creator here does deliberately.
- **Contrarian pattern-interrupt openers.** "Your discipline isn't the
  problem. Your calendar is just better run than your training." is
  structurally identical to Sapp's "nice sales reps make no money" —
  same mechanic (state the opposite of the expected wisdom), zero hype,
  zero incompatibility with brand voice. Keep doing this deliberately.
- **Specificity as the trust lever, applied to process instead of proof.**
  Since Vintus can't use dollar figures, testimonials, or follower counts as
  proof, the honest substitute is *process specificity* — "I ask three
  questions before I program week one," "sleep, then stress, then load, in
  that order" (already present in the calendar, Days 10/21/28). This is the
  same underlying mechanic as Hormozi's numbered frameworks, aimed at
  demonstrating rigor instead of manufacturing urgency. This should be
  named explicitly as Vintus's substitute for the proof these other
  accounts lean on, not left implicit.
- **Identity/self-selection framing, used as repeated brand language, not a
  one-time capstone line.** CLAUDE.md's positioning sentence ("We build
  performance systems for people whose careers don't allow average health")
  currently appears once, on Day 30, as a closer. Sapp and Hormozi both get
  recall from *repeating* a branded phrase across many pieces of content,
  not saving it for one moment. Worth Anthony's explicit decision (not this
  session's call) on whether a repeatable in-group phrase — the positioning
  sentence, or something like "the operator standard" — should recur 3-4
  times across the 30 days instead of landing once at the end.
- **Restrained CTA presence — some, not none.** None of the well-documented
  accounts run at genuinely zero CTA for an extended stretch, even in
  pure-value content — there's almost always a minimal next step (follow,
  bio link). The current calendar's Gaps section already flags "no
  direct-offer or objection-handling content" as intentional for a
  pure-authority month. This research supports revisiting that specifically
  for the back end of the month (Days 25-30) with a soft, low-friction next
  step rather than zero action across all 30 days — not a hard pitch, just
  removing the total absence of a next step.
- **Honest behind-the-scenes vulnerability, not fabricated struggle.**
  Georgas's "Behind the Scenes" format (showing real unfinished work, real
  ups and downs) is usable inside Hybrid Performance content specifically —
  an actual hard training day, not a manufactured comeback narrative — since
  it's a real, undramatized moment rather than invented proof.
