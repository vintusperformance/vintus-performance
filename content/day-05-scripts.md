# Day 5 Scripts — Leadership Through Health

**Angle:** The health decision that tells your team more about your standards than any performance review
**Platforms:** IG + TikTok, plus a LinkedIn variant (script 8)
**Format:** Talking-head, serious tone
**Branded phrase (1 of 4 — Days 5/15/22/30):** close on "We build performance systems for people whose careers don't allow average health." Used in scripts 4 and 8, where it lands as the natural closing line rather than being wedged in everywhere.
**Hook types covered:** blunt statement, direct question, scenario, number-framed, contrarian claim, analogy

Read once, speak into the camera. No stage directions, no hashtags.

---

### 1. Blunt statement hook

Your team knows which meeting you cancelled your workout for.

They don't say it. They notice it.

Standards aren't what you write in the handbook. They're what people watch you protect when it's inconvenient.

---

### 2. Direct question hook

What does your calendar say about your standards?

Not your values page. Your actual calendar.

If the only thing that ever gets moved is the block that keeps you functional, your team has already read that.

They're running the same math on their own week.

---

### 3. Scenario hook

You tell your team to protect their focus time.

Then you eat lunch at your desk, take the 6pm call, and answer email at eleven.

They believe what you do. Not what you said in the all-hands.

---

### 4. Contrarian claim hook — lead with this one

Your team doesn't need you at a hundred percent.

They need you predictable.

The cost of a leader running on four hours of sleep isn't the bad day. It's that nobody can tell which version of you is walking into the room.

Consistency is the deliverable. We build performance systems for people whose careers don't allow average health.

---

### 5. Number-framed hook

Before I change anything in a client's week, I ask three things. When do you decide. When do you present. When do you travel.

The training gets built around those, not the other way around.

Because a plan that collapses the week you actually need it isn't a plan.

---

### 6. Scenario hook

Two leaders, same quarter, same pressure.

One is sharp Monday and gone by Thursday. The other is the same person all five days.

The second one isn't more talented. Their week is built. The first one's is improvised.

---

### 7. Analogy hook

You wouldn't let your company run without a maintenance schedule on the thing that produces the revenue.

You are the thing that produces the revenue.

Your only competition is yesterday.

---

### 8. LinkedIn variant — reflective register, text-first repost or same video

There's a version of leadership that reads as commitment and is actually just poor planning.

Skipping the workout for the meeting. Taking the call at dinner. Being the first one on and the last one off, every week, indefinitely.

Your team is watching, and they are drawing a conclusion. Not "she's dedicated." Closer to "this is what it costs to be senior here."

Then they either accept that price or quietly decide they don't want your job.

The leaders I work with treat their own capacity as infrastructure, not as something to spend for optics. It changes what their team believes is required.

We build performance systems for people whose careers don't allow average health.
