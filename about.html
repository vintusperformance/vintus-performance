The Journey

From Soccer Fields
to the Ironman Course

Anthony Santos grew up a competitive soccer player — and when high school ended, so did the structure of team sports. Most people drift at that point. Anthony accelerated. He discovered the world of hybrid training: combining strength work with serious endurance, pushing the body in every direction at once.

At 18 years old, he stood at the start line of his first IRONMAN 70.3 — 1.2 miles of swimming, 56 miles of cycling, 13.1 miles of running. He finished. What most people spend years working toward, he did in his first year of serious endurance training.

Then, in February 2025, everything stopped. A fibula spiral fracture during training. Two surgeries. Months of recovery. He was 19 years old, sidelined from the sport that had become his identity, unable to run, unable to compete, watching the full IRONMAN he'd been building toward disappear from the calendar.

He didn't quit. He pivoted. The year off the course became the year he built Vintus Performance — channeling everything he knew about discipline, structure, and resilience into a coaching system that could help others achieve what he'd achieved, without having to learn every lesson the hard way.

In 2026, he came back. Second IRONMAN 70.3 — 5 hours and 31 minutes. And on July 19, 2026, he'll cross the finish line of a full IRONMAN in Lake Placid, New York. Before his 21st birthday.
